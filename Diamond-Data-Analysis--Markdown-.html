Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

1. Distribution of Diamond Prices

Que1 - What factors affect diamond price?

ggplot(diamonds_clean, aes(x = price)) +
  geom_histogram(bins = 50, fill = "steelblue") +
  labs(
    title = "Distribution of Diamond Prices",
    x = "Price",
    y = "Frequency"
  )

Key Insights:

  • The distribution is right-skewed, meaning most diamonds are lower-priced, while only a few diamonds are extremely expensive.
  • Most diamonds are concentrated in the lower price range, showing that premium-quality diamonds are relatively rare.
  • The presence of very high-priced diamonds suggests that features like larger carat size, better cut, clarity, and color quality increase price significantly.

2. Relationship Between Carat and Price

Que2 - Does carat strongly influence price?

ggplot(diamonds_clean, aes(x = carat, y = price)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.3, color = "steelblue") +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = TRUE, color = "red") +
  labs(
    title = "Relationship Between Carat and Price",
    x = "Carat (Size)",
    y = "Price (USD)"
  )
## `geom_smooth()` using formula = 'y ~ x'

Key Insights:

  • The scatter plot shows a strong positive relationship between carat and price.
  • As carat size increases, diamond price generally increases significantly.
  • The upward trend line confirms that larger diamonds tend to be more expensive.
  • Carat appears to be one of the most influential factors in determining diamond price.

3. Average Price by Cut

Que3 - Which cut category has highest average price?

diamonds_clean %>%
  group_by(cut) %>%
  summarise(avg_price = mean(price)) %>%
  ggplot(aes(x = cut, y = avg_price, fill = cut)) +
  geom_col() +
  labs(
    title = "Average Diamond Price by Cut",
    x = "Cut",
    y = "Average Price (USD)"
  )

Key Insights:

  • The Premium cut category has the highest average diamond price among all cut types.
  • Fair cut diamonds also show relatively high average prices, possibly due to larger carat sizes in that category.
  • Ideal cut diamonds have a lower average price compared to Premium and Fair cuts.
  • This suggests that cut quality alone does not determine price. Factors like carat, clarity, and color also strongly influence pricing.

1. Correlation Test Between Carat and Price

Que - Is there a strong relationship between carat and price?

H0: No linear relationship between Carat and Price H1: Relationship exists

cor.test(
  diamonds_clean$carat,
  diamonds_clean$price
)
## 
##  Pearson's product-moment correlation
## 
## data:  diamonds_clean$carat and diamonds_clean$price
## t = 550.39, df = 53773, p-value < 0.00000000000000022
## alternative hypothesis: true correlation is not equal to 0
## 95 percent confidence interval:
##  0.9202643 0.9228128
## sample estimates:
##       cor 
## 0.9215485

Interpretation:

The correlation coefficient is: r = 0.9215

This indicates a very strong positive correlation between carat and price. (Larger diamonds tend to be much more expensive.)

  • As the carat size increases, the price of diamonds also increases significantly.
  • The p-value is extremely small (p < 0.00000000000000022), meaning the relationship is statistically significant.
  • Since the p-value is far below 0.05 (significance level), we reject the null hypothesis and conclude that carat and price are significantly related.
  • The 95% confidence interval (0.9203 to 0.9228) confirms that the true correlation is consistently very strong.

2. Chi-Square Test of Independence Between Cut and Color

Que - Is there a significant relationship between two categorical variables - Cut and Color?

H0: Variables are Independent (No relationship) H1: Variables are Dependent (Significant Relationship)

chisq.test(
  diamonds_clean$cut,
  diamonds_clean$color
)
## 
##  Pearson's Chi-squared test
## 
## data:  diamonds_clean$cut and diamonds_clean$color
## X-squared = 306.7, df = 24, p-value < 0.00000000000000022

Interpretation:

  • The p-value is extremely small (p < 0.00000000000000022), which is far below 0.05.

  • Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis of independence.

  • This means there is a statistically significant relationship between diamond cut and color.

  • Degree of Freedom (df): [Color = 7 Categories, Cut = 5 Categories] therefore, df = (7-1) x (5-1) = 6 x 4 = 24

3. One-Way ANOVA for Price Differences Across Cut Categories

Que - Do different cut categories have significantly different prices?

H0: The mean diamond prices are equal across all cut categories. H1: At least one cut category has a different mean diamond price.

anova_model <- aov(
  price ~ cut,
  data = diamonds_clean
)

summary(anova_model)
##                Df       Sum Sq    Mean Sq F value              Pr(>F)    
## cut             4  10788727783 2697181946   171.9 <0.0000000000000002 ***
## Residuals   53770 843548279132   15688084                                
## ---
## Signif. codes:  0 '***' 0.001 '**' 0.01 '*' 0.05 '.' 0.1 ' ' 1

Interpretation:

This is a One-Way ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) test.

Independent Variable: Cut Dependent Variable: Price

The test compares the mean prices across different cut categories.

  • The p-value is extremely small (p < 0.0000000000000002), which is far below 0.05.
  • Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis that all cut categories have the same average price.
  • This means there are significant differences in average diamond prices across cut categories.
  • The high F-value (171.9) indicates that variation in price between cut groups is much larger than variation within groups.
